# NAMA — Lesson 4: Running a Reading Club

**Audience:** Teachers
**Lesson outcome:** Plan, organise and improve a school reading club.

## Pure narration

Welcome

Let's analyse our reading club. A strength is our keen volunteers. A weakness is that we have little time. An opportunity is the new library. A threat is the busy exam season.

Now we sort the tasks by impact and effort. Choosing good books has high impact and low effort, so we do it first. Training volunteers has high impact but high effort, so we plan it. Making posters is low impact and low effort, so we share it. A big launch party is low impact and high effort, so we drop it.

Our first meeting was quiet. Why? Few students came. Why? They did not know about it. Why? We only told one class. Why? We had no plan for sharing news.

Here are our rules for noise. Stop when someone is reading aloud. Be careful with side chats. Go ahead and discuss freely in groups.

How ready are we? The levels are not ready, getting there, almost, and ready. Right now, we are almost there.

We rated our club on four things out of 10. Books scored 8. Space scored 6. Volunteers scored 9. Promotion scored 4.

Here is how the ideas connect. Reading builds vocabulary. Vocabulary improves writing. Discussion deepens reading.

A reading habit is a chain. It links choosing a book, making time, reading daily, and talking about it. The weakest link is usually time.

One small action sets off a chain reaction. A good first book sparks interest. Interest leads to more reading. More reading builds confidence. Confidence creates lifelong readers.

When one student shares a book, the effect spreads. First a friend reads it, then the class talks about it, and soon the whole school knows it.

Some things we control, like our book choices and our meeting time. Some things we influence, like student interest. And some things only concern us, like the exam timetable.

Many ideas come in: student requests, teacher tips, and library lists. They narrow to one focus: the book of the month. From that come two outcomes: shared reading and lively talks.

Let's zoom in. The big picture is a reading culture. Inside it is our club. And inside the club, it is one good conversation about a book.

We return to the same skill each term, but deeper. In term one, we retell stories. In term two, we compare stories. In term three, we judge stories.

Who does what? The teacher picks the books. The students read them. The librarian orders copies. The students then lead the talk.

Here is where our tasks stand. To do: make a sign up sheet. Doing: choose the first book. Done: book the room.

Here is the plan. Planning runs from week 1 to week 2. Reading runs from week 2 to week 5. The final talk is in week 6.

Our week looks like this. On Monday, we choose. On Wednesday, we read together. On Friday, we talk.

Each meeting lasts one hour. We spend 10 minutes on a warm up, 30 minutes reading, and 20 minutes talking.

Then, only a few students read for fun. Now, our club meets every week. Next, we want a reading corner in every class.

A good reader grows in three ways. With the head, they understand stories. With the heart, they enjoy them. With the hands, they share them.

Four values guide our club. Curiosity points north. Kindness points east. Effort points south. And fun points west.

Thank you for watching. See you in the next lesson.
