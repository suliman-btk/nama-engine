# NAMA — Lesson 3: Helping Learners Remember

**Audience:** Teachers
**Lesson outcome:** Use recall, spacing and feedback so that learners remember what they learn.

## Pure narration

Welcome

Here is something surprising. Learners who test themselves remember far more than learners who simply read their notes again.

This lesson has four parts: recall, practice, feedback, and review. We are starting with part one, recall.

Two words will help us. Retrieval means pulling a memory back out. Spacing means leaving time between practice sessions.

Try this card. What is the forgetting curve? It is the way memories fade quickly unless we review them.

Complete the sentence: we remember what we practise retrieving.

Match each habit with its benefit. Quizzing builds recall. Spacing beats cramming. Mixing topics sharpens choices.

Put these steps in order. First, read the text. Second, close the book. Third, write what you remember. Last, check your notes.

Two groups studied the same text. One group reread it, and the other group tested themselves. A week later, which group remembered more? The group that tested themselves remembered much more.

Learners often ask: how often should I review? Review a little every few days. And how long should a session be? About twenty minutes works well.

Here is a short conversation. Sara asks: should I reread my notes? Omar answers: try to recall them first. Sara says: that sounds harder. Omar replies: harder is better for memory.

In a class of ten, eight learners said quizzes helped them remember.

In our example, average scores rose from 55 percent before weekly quizzes to 78 percent after them.

Learners rated two study habits. Rereading scored 2 out of 5. Self quizzing scored 4 out of 5.

In this example, here is how much learners remembered after one week. With rereading, 30 percent. With highlighting, 25 percent. With self testing, 60 percent. Testing wins.

Why do learners forget? There is no review, sessions are too long, they get little sleep, and they feel stressed. Together, these lead to forgetting.

The recipe is simple. Recall plus spacing plus feedback equals lasting memory.

To get from forgetting to remembering, we cross a bridge of small steps: short quizzes, spaced reviews, and quick feedback.

Memory is a puzzle with three pieces: attention, practice, and sleep. When they fit together, learning lasts.

Everything grows from one root: curiosity. From it grow questions, notes, discussions, and projects.

Thank you for watching. In the next lesson, we explore feedback that works: what to say and when to say it.
